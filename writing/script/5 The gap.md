[ANIM: instagram]

I think there's something deeper underneath. This part is speculation.

You sit down to study. You know that's what you want. And a few minutes later, you're on Instagram, scrolling. Nobody decided that. It happens without thinking.

And then, at some point, maybe after ten seconds, maybe after forty minutes, something kicks in. You realise: I wanted to study. And you go back.

[ANIM: the gap]

Two things happened there. One part of you runs by itself: habits, impulses, the next obvious thing. And another part notices. It looks at what you're doing and turns you around.

I'd argue that what we call consciousness is exactly this supervising part. Judging what we're doing right now against what we want to do with our lives, our goals. And readjusting.

And the time between drifting off and noticing: that's the gap.

[ANIM: the gap at every scale]

And it's not only about Instagram. You plan to do the one important thing today, answer a few mails first, and at six in the evening you notice it didn't happen. Or over years: you study what made sense, take the job that came next, and one day you ask yourself whether this is what you wanted.

Same move. Only the gap gets longer.

[ANIM: meditation shortens the gap]

The problem is, this supervisor is weak. One study found that our minds wander almost half of the time we're awake. [check: Killingsworth and Gilbert, 2010]

But it can be trained. That's what meditation is. You sit, you focus on your breath, your mind wanders off, you notice, and you come back. That moment of noticing is the exercise. And every time, the gap gets a little shorter. [check: Hasenkamp et al., 2012, on the wander, notice, return cycle]

[ANIM: the file closes the gap]

And that's what the file does for my projects. In my head, I jump to solutions, or the AI does, and it can take weeks until I notice. On a page, it gets noticed much earlier. The page asks: why are we doing this again? It's a supervisor outside my head. And it doesn't get tired.
