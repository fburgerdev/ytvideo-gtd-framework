# The five stages

[CARD: The five stages]

[ANIM: five lines on the page | tail=2]

Then I came across a model from Getting Things Done, a book by David Allen. He describes how our mind plans, when it plans well. Five stages. [check: Allen calls it the natural planning model]

Purpose. Why am I doing this?

Vision. What does it look like when it worked?

Brainstorming. Everything that comes to mind, without judging.

Organising. Giving the pile a shape.

And next actions. What is the very next thing I do?

[ANIM: we start in the middle]

Allen says we do this naturally for small things, like going out for dinner. But as soon as something feels important, we skip the first stages. We start in the middle, with organising and solutions, because that feels productive. He calls it reactive planning. [check: the dinner example and the term, in the book]

[ANIM: example, the question | stage=4]

Here's an example. Say you run a student group, and too few people come to your meetings. So you ask: how do we get more people?

You'll get posters. Instagram. Free pizza. A stall on campus. All reasonable.

[ANIM: example, purpose | stage=1]

Now start at the top instead. Why do you want more people? Maybe the honest answer is: because three of us do all the work, and we're tired. Why does that matter? Because otherwise the group dies when we leave.

Look what happened. You don't need more people at meetings. You need two or three who take over real work. Posters and pizza just fell off the table.

[ANIM: example, vision | stage=2]

Then the vision. Assume it worked. What do you see? Not "the group is doing well". Something specific enough to be wrong. A Tuesday in June, and someone else is running the meeting, while you sit in the back.

[ANIM: example, brainstorm and organise | stage=3]

Only now you brainstorm. And the ideas are different, because they know where they're going.

Then you sort them: what belongs together, what comes first.

[ANIM: example, next action | stage=5]

And at the end, one next action. Something so concrete that somebody else could do it without deciding anything. Write to one person, and ask if she wants to plan the next meeting with you.

[ANIM: the file between us]

I wrote these five stages into one file, and told my AI: this is how we think together. Stay in a stage until I move on. And if I'm stuck, point me back up.

I expected a small improvement. It was another leap.

[ANIM: stuck, go one up]

The part I use most is this. When something is stuck, the problem is usually one stage higher. No ideas? The vision isn't vivid enough. Vision vague? The purpose isn't clear. And when everything is too much, go all the way down: what's the next action?
